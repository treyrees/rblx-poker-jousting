## Context

"Above the opponent" needed a meaning. The review offered (a) full poker ranking, with only
exact ties level, or (b) rungs as categories, with the same category level. A deal sim found
that when both riders own a trick, about 76% of the time it is the same category. Because
tricks are built to unhorse (0018), two level tricks that both applied would unhorse each
other, and §2's mutual-unhorse rule (more Posture before the pass) would decide the hand,
not the joust.

## Decision

Trey: "6.2b. we have a good gameplay layer, lets use it to break common ties where the common
cards provide both players a similar trick."

On the mutual-unhorse problem, Trey: "i think that when both players have a trick, it
returns to being numerical. this should give the strong edge either back to who has the
stronger of the shared trick, who gets it first before the other, with some outcome
determined by the skill too. long way to say yes i dont think a trick should be auto unhorse
because we have good poker tiebreaking available to us."

- The rungs are straight, flush, full house, quads, straight flush. The royal flush is the
  top straight flush. Numeric hands are below the ladder.
- Riders on the same rung are level, whatever their ranks within it.
- Level tricks clash: both cancel for that pass, it resolves as numeric, and both are spent.
  This extends the old clash rule from identical tricks to all level tricks.
- The edge between level tricks comes from who reached the rung first, the stronger hand at
  the showdown knockdown, and the dial.

## Consequences

- §6 gains *The trick ladder*; *Trick vs trick* rewritten; §8's clash beat is for level
  tricks.
- Trey's "returns to being numerical" answered the review's question about level tricks, and
  is recorded for them. Tricks on different rungs follow 0020.
